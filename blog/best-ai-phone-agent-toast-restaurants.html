Best AI Phone Agent for Toast Restaurants (October 2026)

If your restaurant runs on Toast, most of your orders already flow into one system: the counter, Toast Online Ordering, delivery partners and the kitchen display. The phone is the exception. It still depends on whoever is free to pick up, and at 6:30 on a Friday that's usually nobody.

An AI phone agent closes that gap by answering every call and sending phone orders into Toast like any other ticket. But “works with Toast” can mean very different things from one vendor to the next. This guide covers what a real Toast integration looks like, the Toast-specific details to test, how pricing compares and the questions to ask before you sign.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for Toast?

The best AI phone agent for a Toast restaurant does five things well:

  1. Sends phone orders directly into Toast, so they fire to your KDS or printers with no re-keying.
  2. Syncs your Toast menu automatically, including modifier groups, prices and items you've 86'd.
  3. Upsells on every order based on what the guest already picked.
  4. Hands off to your team smoothly when a guest needs a person.
  5. Charges a predictable price that doesn't grow every time you get busier.

What “Toast Integration” Should Actually Mean

Ask every vendor which of these three levels they actually deliver.

1. Text-to-order links

The agent answers, then texts the guest a link to your Toast Online Ordering page. It's easy and better than a missed call, but the guest still has to finish the order on their phone, and some won't.

2. Direct order entry into Toast

The agent takes the whole order in conversation, including modifiers, special instructions, the guest's name and pickup time, and sends it into Toast as a ticket. Your kitchen sees it exactly like a counter or online order. This is the level that turns missed calls into revenue.

3. Real-time menu sync

The agent reads your menu from Toast and keeps up when you change it. If you raise a price, add a special or mark an item out of stock, callers hear the change without anyone touching a second system.

The strongest setups do all three: direct ordering, a text link as a fallback for guests who prefer it, and a menu that always matches Toast.

Toast Details Worth Testing in a Demo

Toast menus tend to be more detailed than a simple item list. These are the places where weak integrations break:

  • Modifier groups: required choices (size, temperature, bread), optional add-ons and nested modifiers. Order your most complicated item and check the ticket.
  • Out-of-stock items: 86 something in Toast, then call and try to order it. The agent should offer an alternative, not take the order.
  • Menus that change by time of day: breakfast, lunch, happy hour and late night. The agent shouldn't sell breakfast burritos at 8 p.m.
  • Pickup times: are quoted times realistic for your kitchen on a busy night, or a fixed number?
  • Multiple locations: if each store has its own Toast menu and hours, the agent should follow each one.
  • Payment: does the guest pay at pickup, or get a secure link to pay by text? Ask how paid orders show up in Toast.
  • Reporting: can you tell phone orders apart from counter and online orders, so you know what the agent is actually bringing in?

What About Toast's Own AI?

Toast has been adding AI tools under the Toast IQ name. So far they focus mainly on marketing, labor scheduling, reviews and menu content, which help you run the business behind the counter. Answering calls and taking orders by voice is a different job, and it's usually handled by a dedicated AI phone agent that connects to Toast. The two work side by side: Toast stays your system of record, and the phone agent feeds it orders.

Pricing Models Compared

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Cheap when you're slow, expensive once it works.
  • Per minute or per call: your bill climbs on your busiest days, exactly when the agent is most useful.
  • Flat monthly fee: one price no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget, and every extra order is upside.

Whatever the model, ask for the total at your real call volume, including setup fees, call caps and charges per location. Our guide to what AI phone answering costs walks through the math.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Palona AI, SoundHound, Slang AI, ConverseNow or anyone else, these questions separate a real Toast integration from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go directly into Toast, or does the guest get a link?
  2. Does the menu sync from Toast automatically, including modifier groups and 86'd items?
  3. How do you handle menus that change by time of day?
  4. How are pickup times quoted?
  5. How does payment work, and how do paid orders appear in Toast?
  6. What happens when the guest asks for a person? Do you brief my staff first?
  7. Is there a cap on calls, or extra charges when we're busy?
  8. Do you take a percentage of my orders?
  9. Does it handle Spanish?
  10. Who sets up the Toast connection, and how long until we're live?

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an AI phone agent work with Toast?

Yes. The best AI phone agents connect to Toast so phone orders arrive as Toast tickets, and menu changes you make in Toast carry over to the agent automatically. Ask each vendor whether orders go directly into Toast or the guest only gets a link to your Toast online ordering page.

Will AI phone orders show up on my Toast KDS?

With direct ordering, yes. The order lands in Toast like any other ticket, so it routes to your kitchen display or printers the way you already have them set up. Nobody re-keys it.

Can an AI phone agent handle Toast modifier groups?

A good one can. It should read your modifier groups from Toast, ask for required choices like size or temperature, and offer optional add-ons. Test it on your most complicated item during the demo.

What happens when we 86 an item in Toast?

With real-time menu sync, an item you mark out of stock in Toast stops being offered to callers. Without sync, someone has to update the agent by hand, and sooner or later a guest orders something you don't have.
