Thanksgiving Restaurant Phone Orders: How to Handle the Busiest Week of the Year (2026 Guide)

For a lot of restaurants, Thanksgiving isn't one day. It's a three-week rush that ends with the busiest 48 hours of the year. Think of a comfort-food brand known for its sides: turkey dinners for eight, mac and cheese and collards by the half pan, cornbread, a few hundred pies. Preorders start weeks ahead. The dining room is full on Wednesday night, and on Thursday it's full of families who didn't want to cook. In between, a line of guests is picking up the dinners they ordered.

And the phone rings the entire time. A guest wants to know if the feast feeds ten. Another wants to swap the green beans. Someone is calling at 10:30 at night, after the kids are in bed, to finally place the order they've been meaning to place for a week. Someone else is running late for pickup. Every one of those calls lands on a team that is already serving a full house. This guide covers how to set up the phone for Thanksgiving week: the menu, the answers, the orders at all hours, payment, pickup day, sell-outs and what to do if you're reading this the week of.

Quick Answer: How Should Restaurants Handle Thanksgiving Phone Orders?

  1. Set up the holiday menu as its own menu, with clear packages, serving sizes, add-ons and pickup times.
  2. Write down the answers to the ten questions everyone asks, and use them on every channel.
  3. Take orders at any hour, because holiday orders come in early in the morning, late at night and on weekends.
  4. Collect payment and confirm by text when the order is placed.
  5. Keep the phone off the floor, so the team serving guests in the building isn't also running the phone.
  6. Plan for pickup day and sell-out day before they happen.

Why Thanksgiving Is Different on the Phone

  • Orders come in at all hours. Holiday meals get planned around family schedules, not your business hours. Guests call before work, on their lunch break, late at night and on Sunday afternoon. A call that goes to voicemail at 9:45 p.m. is often an order that goes to a grocery store instead.
  • The phone is busiest when the dining room is busiest. The week of Thanksgiving, your evening rush and your phone rush happen at the same time, and the person at the host stand can't seat a party of six and walk a caller through the feast menu at once.
  • The orders are big and detailed. A holiday dinner is a package, a size, a choice of sides, extra sides by the pan, pies, a pickup date and a pickup time. Getting one detail wrong means a family short a side on Thanksgiving.
  • Everyone asks the same questions. Are you open Thursday? How many does it feed? Is it hot or do I heat it up? When's the last day to order? Each answer takes a minute, and you'll give it hundreds of times.
  • Pickup day brings its own calls. Running late, can I change my time, which door do I use, is my order ready, I think I forgot to order the pie.
  • Staffing is tight. Team members want the holiday off, and the ones who are working are needed on the floor and in the kitchen, not on the phone.

Step 1: Build the Holiday Menu Before Guests Start Asking

Most holiday phone problems start as menu problems. Before you take the first order, set up Thanksgiving as its own menu, separate from your everyday one, in your POS and online ordering:

  • Packages with clear serving sizes: “serves 4–6” and “serves 8–10,” not “small” and “large.”
  • Exactly what's included: the protein, how many sides and which ones, rolls or cornbread, gravy, dessert.
  • Choices and swaps: which sides can be swapped, and whether a swap costs extra. Our guide to menu modifiers in AI voice ordering covers how to set these up so phone orders come out right.
  • Add-ons: extra sides by the pan, whole pies, extra gravy, drinks by the gallon.
  • Pickup dates and time slots, with a limit on how many orders each slot can take, so the kitchen isn't handing out 200 dinners in the same half hour.

When the menu is set up this way, anyone who answers the phone, a person or an AI phone agent, is working from the same list, and the orders that land in your kitchen look the same no matter how they came in.

Step 2: Write Down the Answers to the Ten Questions Everyone Asks

Thanksgiving calls are repetitive, which is good news: you can answer most of them before they're asked. Write these down, get them approved and put them on your website, your phone greeting and in whatever answers your phone:

  1. Are you open on Thanksgiving, and what are the hours Wednesday, Thursday and Friday?
  2. Is the dining room taking reservations or walk-ins on Thanksgiving?
  3. What's included in each holiday meal, and how many people does it serve?
  4. Does it come hot, or is it ready to reheat? Where are the heating instructions?
  5. What's the last day to order?
  6. What are the pickup times, and where exactly do I pick up?
  7. Can I swap a side or add extra sides and pies?
  8. What allergens are in the dishes, and is anything gluten-free or vegetarian?
  9. Do I pay now or at pickup, and what if I need to cancel?
  10. Do you deliver, or is it pickup only?

If you run more than one location, keep one set of answers for the group and a short list of differences for each store, such as hours and pickup instructions. Our guide to running one phone standard across every store walks through how.

Step 3: Take Orders at Any Hour

The guest who calls at 10:30 p.m. on a Sunday is ready to order right now. If nobody answers, there's a good chance they don't call back. During Thanksgiving season, every hour your phone isn't taking orders is an hour of holiday orders you aren't getting. Give guests two ways to order on every call, at any hour:

  • Take the order on the call, with the package, sides, add-ons, pickup date and pickup time, sent straight into your POS.
  • Or text the guest a link to your holiday ordering page, for guests who'd rather tap through the menu.

Either way, read the order back before the call ends. Holiday orders are expensive and hard to fix on Thursday morning.

Step 4: Collect Payment and Confirm by Text

A no-show on a normal Tuesday is a lost ticket. A no-show on a Thanksgiving dinner is a turkey, four sides and a pie you already made. Collecting payment when the order is placed protects you, and it means pickup is fast: the guest grabs the bag and goes.

Don't ask anyone to read a card number over the phone in a loud restaurant. Text the guest a secure payment link instead. Then send a text confirmation with everything they'll need on the day: what they ordered, the pickup date and time, the address and where to go when they arrive. That text answers half of the pickup-day calls before they happen.

Step 5: Keep the Phone Off the Floor

On Wednesday night and Thursday, your team's job is the guests in the building. Every minute a host or server spends on the phone is a minute a table waits. Decide ahead of time which calls actually need someone at the store:

  • Handled without the store: questions, hours, menu details, new holiday orders, payment links and order confirmations.
  • Sent to a person: a guest who asks for one, a problem with an order that's already been made and large same-day orders that the kitchen has to approve.
  • Sent in writing: complaints, with the guest's details and the full call, so a manager can follow up after the rush.
  • Sent to the catering team: office parties and large group orders that aren't part of the Thanksgiving menu.

When a call does go to the store, the person picking up should already know why the guest is calling. Our guide to AI-to-human handoffs covers how to set that up.

Step 6: Plan for Pickup Day

For many restaurants, the Wednesday before Thanksgiving and Thursday morning are the busiest pickup hours of the year. The phone calls on those days are predictable, so plan for them:

  • “I'm running late.” Decide how long you'll hold an order and what happens after that.
  • “Can I change my pickup time?” Decide whether changes are allowed on the day, and into which time slots.
  • “Where do I go?” A separate pickup table or door, explained in the confirmation text and on the phone.
  • “I forgot the pie.” Know which add-ons you can still sell on the day, and suggest them. It's an easy sale.
  • “Something's missing from my order.” Have a manager ready to fix it quickly, because on Thanksgiving it can't wait until Monday.

Put one person in charge of pickup each shift, with a printed list of orders by time slot.

Step 7: Plan for the Cutoff and the Sell-Out

At some point you'll stop taking holiday orders, either because you've hit your cutoff or because the kitchen is full. The calls don't stop when the orders do. The day you close orders:

  • Update the answer everywhere at once, on your website, your online ordering and your phone, so nobody is told they can still order.
  • Give callers something else: sides and pies that are still available, a dine-in table if you have one, or your holiday menu for December.
  • Keep a waitlist if cancellations are likely, and call the guests on it back in order.

Already Thanksgiving Week? Do These Five Things Today

If you're reading this the Monday of Thanksgiving week, there's still time to fix the parts that matter most:

  1. Record a holiday greeting with your Thanksgiving hours and order deadline. If you don't have time to record one, we can record it for you.
  2. Print the answers to the ten questions above and tape them next to every phone.
  3. Pick one person per shift to own the phone and pickup, so it isn't whoever happens to be closest.
  4. Text every guest who has ordered their pickup time and where to go.
  5. Decide your sell-out answer now, so nobody has to make it up on the call.

A Day-by-Day Checklist for Thanksgiving Week

  • Two to three weeks out: holiday menu live in the POS and online, answers written, preorders open, holiday greeting recorded.
  • The weekend before: check orders against kitchen capacity for each pickup slot. Expect late-night and weekend calls.
  • Monday and Tuesday: the last big wave of orders. Close orders on time and update every channel the same hour.
  • Wednesday: pickup day for many guests, and one of the busiest dining room nights of the year. Phones off the floor, one person on pickup.
  • Thursday: morning pickups, a full dining room, a short team. Only calls that truly need a person should reach the store.
  • Friday and the weekend: follow up on every complaint, and start taking orders for December.

After Thanksgiving: Look at the Calls

The week after the holiday is the best time to learn from it, while it's fresh. Look at how many calls you got each day and hour, how many turned into orders, which questions came up most, which calls had to go to a person and why, and what went wrong at pickup. Then fix it before the next holiday. The December rush is next: office parties, holiday catering and Christmas Eve dinners. Questions to Ask Any AI Phone Agent Vendor About Holidays

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Palona AI, Slang AI, ConverseNow, SoundHound or anyone else, ask:

  1. Can the agent take orders from a separate holiday menu, with packages, sides and add-ons?
  2. Can it take a pickup date and time days in advance, and send the order into our POS?
  3. How many calls can it answer at the same time on our busiest night?
  4. Can it text guests a secure payment link and an order confirmation?
  5. How quickly can we change holiday hours, the order deadline or a sold-out item, and can we do it ourselves?
  6. Can each location have its own holiday hours and pickup instructions?
  7. Which calls go to our team, and what does our team hear before the call connects?
  8. How is it priced during our busiest weeks? Per minute, per call, a commission on orders or a flat fee?

Frequently Asked Questions

When should restaurants start taking Thanksgiving preorders?

Many restaurants open Thanksgiving preorders in early November and close them a few days before the holiday, so the kitchen knows how many turkeys, sides and pies to prep. Whatever dates you pick, put the cutoff on every channel, including the phone, and say it on every call.

How can a restaurant take Thanksgiving phone orders when the dining room is full?

Move the phone away from the people serving guests. An AI phone agent can answer every call, including several at once, take the holiday order or text the guest an ordering link, and send only the calls that truly need a person to the store. That way the host stand and the kitchen stay focused on the guests in the building.

Should restaurants take payment up front for Thanksgiving orders?

Usually, yes. Collecting payment when the order is placed cuts down on no-shows, which matter much more when the food is a whole holiday meal prepared for one specific pickup time. Texting the guest a secure payment link means no one has to read a card number over the phone during the rush.

What do guests ask most when they call a restaurant about Thanksgiving?

Whether you're open on Thanksgiving and what the hours are, what's included in each holiday meal, how many people it serves, whether it comes hot or ready to reheat, the order deadline, the pickup times and where to pick up, allergens and substitutions, and whether they can still get a table. Write those answers down before the week starts.

What should a restaurant do when Thanksgiving orders sell out?

Update the answer everywhere at once, including the phone, so guests aren't told they can still order. Then give callers something else: sides or pies that are still available, a table if you have one, or your holiday menu for the weeks ahead.
