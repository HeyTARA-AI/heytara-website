Best AI Phone Agent for NCR Aloha Restaurants (October 2026)

NCR Aloha runs some of the busiest restaurant groups in the country. It’s built for volume: fast tickets, deep modifier menus and the same setup rolled out across dozens or hundreds of stores. The phone is the one place that volume still leaks. When a call comes in at 6:30 on a Friday, the person who answers is usually also running the register or expo, and the order ends up on a notepad, gets rung in by hand or never gets taken at all.

AI phone agents promise to fix that, but Aloha is where many of them quietly fall short. Aloha is harder to connect to than newer cloud POS systems, so a lot of phone tools stop at texting the guest a link or sending the order to the store to ring in by hand. This guide covers what a real Aloha connection looks like, what to test in a demo, how setup should work, how pricing compares and the questions to ask before you sign.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for Aloha Restaurants?

The best AI phone agent for a restaurant on NCR Aloha does five things well:

  1. Sends phone orders directly into Aloha, so they ring in like any other ticket and nobody re-keys them.
  2. Orders from your real Aloha menu, with the right items, modifiers and prices, and picks up menu changes automatically.
  3. Handles complicated orders in natural conversation: combos, substitutions, add-ons and pickup times.
  4. Works the same way at every location, and across every POS if your group runs more than one.
  5. Charges a predictable price that doesn’t take a cut of every order.

Why Aloha Restaurants Need More Than a Basic Phone Bot

A phone tool built for a single cafe on a newer cloud POS can get away with a lot. An Aloha operation usually can’t:

  • The connection is the hard part. Aloha has been running restaurants for decades, and many stores have years of custom menus, modifier groups and store-level settings built up. A phone agent has to work with that setup as it is, not ask you to rebuild it.
  • Menus are deep. Aloha menus often carry layers of modifiers, combos and sizes. If the phone agent’s menu doesn’t match the POS exactly, tickets ring in wrong or don’t ring in at all.
  • Many stores, many habits. Every location answers the phone differently, and corporate has no view into what happens on those calls or how many go unanswered.
  • Peak hits twice. At the dinner rush, the person on the phone is also on the register or expo. Calls ring out right when guests want to order.
  • Sometimes more than one POS. Groups that run several brands or grew through acquisitions often have Aloha in some stores and something else in others.

What “Works With Aloha” Should Actually Mean

Vendors use the same words for very different things. Ask which of these three levels they actually deliver at your stores.

1. Text-to-order links

The agent answers, then texts the guest a link to your online ordering page. It’s better than a missed call, but the guest has to finish the order on their phone, and some won’t.

2. Orders sent to the store to ring in

The agent takes the order, then sends it to the store by email, a separate tablet or a printer, and someone rings it into Aloha by hand. The call got answered, but you’re still re-keying orders at peak, which is exactly where wrong modifiers, missed items and slow tickets come from.

3. Orders directly into Aloha

The agent takes the whole order from your Aloha menu and sends it straight into Aloha. The kitchen gets a ticket it already knows how to read, with no one re-entering it at the register. This is the level that turns calls into revenue without adding work.

Then ask the one question that settles it: can we watch a phone order ring into Aloha live during the demo?

Details Worth Testing in a Demo

This is where weak integrations show. Bring your real menu and test the orders your stores actually get:

  • Your most complicated item: order it with substitutions and removals, then read the ticket that lands in Aloha.
  • Combos and sizes: order a combo, change the side and upsize the drink. Check that every modifier is a real one from your menu.
  • A menu change: change a price or add an item in Aloha before the demo and see whether the agent offers it correctly.
  • The kitchen ticket: look at how the order prints or shows on your kitchen screen. Your team should be able to read it like any other ticket.
  • Pickup times: ask for a pickup time during your rush and see how the agent handles it.
  • Two locations: if your stores have different menus, prices or hours, call two of them and compare the tickets.
  • Transfers: ask for a manager and check where the call goes and whether the transcript follows.
  • Spanish: if your guests call in Spanish, place an order in Spanish.

How Setup Should Work

Connecting a phone agent to Aloha shouldn’t turn into an IT project for your team. Ask every vendor who does each of these steps, and how long each one takes:

  • Connecting to your Aloha stores and confirming orders flow through correctly before any guest calls.
  • Importing your menu from Aloha, so the agent starts with the same items, modifiers and prices your registers use.
  • Configuring each location: hours, pickup times, upsells, catering, complaints and transfers.
  • Testing end to end with real test calls before going live.

Also ask whether you need new hardware or changes to your Aloha setup. Ideally the answer is no: the agent should work with the Aloha system and phone line you already have. And ask for a firm go-live date for your locations, not a range.

Pricing Models Compared

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Across a busy multi-unit group, that adds up fast, and it grows every time the agent does its job.
  • Per minute or per call: your bill climbs on your busiest days and at your busiest stores, which makes budgeting by location harder.
  • Flat monthly fee per location: one predictable price per store, no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget, and every extra order is upside.

Whatever the model, ask for the total at your real call volume across all your locations, including setup fees, call caps and any extra charges. Our guide to what AI phone answering costs walks through the math.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you’re comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Palona AI, SoundHound, Slang AI, ConverseNow or anyone else, these questions separate a real Aloha integration from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go directly into Aloha, or does someone at the store ring them in?
  2. Can we watch a phone order ring into Aloha live during the demo?
  3. Does the menu come from Aloha, and do changes carry over automatically?
  4. How do you handle our modifiers, combos and substitutions?
  5. How do you handle different menus, prices and hours by location?
  6. When a guest asks for a person, where does the call go?
  7. Do we need new hardware or changes to our Aloha setup?
  8. Is there a cap on calls, or do you take a percentage of our orders?
  9. What if some of our brands or locations use a different POS?
  10. Who handles setup, and when will our locations be live?
