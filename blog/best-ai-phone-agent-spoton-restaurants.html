Best AI Phone Agent for SpotOn Restaurants (November 2026)

SpotOn has become the POS of choice for a lot of full-service restaurants, bars and growing local groups. It runs the counter and the handhelds, and SpotOn's own online ordering and reservations tools mean many guests never have to call. But plenty still do. Regulars call. Large parties call. Anyone with a question about parking, allergens or a catering tray calls. And they call at exactly the moment your host is seating a six-top and the bar is three deep.

An AI phone agent answers those calls, handles the questions and can send phone orders straight into SpotOn. This guide covers what a real SpotOn integration looks like, how to get your SpotOn menu ready for phone orders, what to test in a demo, how pricing works and the questions to ask any vendor before you sign.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for SpotOn?

The best AI phone agent for a SpotOn restaurant does five things well:

  1. Sends phone orders directly into SpotOn as tickets your kitchen already knows how to read.
  2. Stays in sync with your SpotOn menu, so price changes and 86'd items never reach a guest.
  3. Gets your modifiers right: sizes, temperatures, add-ons, “no onions” and “dressing on the side.”
  4. Answers every call, including several at once, and hands off to a person smoothly when needed.
  5. Charges a flat, predictable price per location, not a cut of every order.

What “Works With SpotOn” Should Actually Mean

Almost every vendor says they work with SpotOn. Ask which of these three levels they actually run for SpotOn restaurants today, not on a roadmap:

1. A link to your online ordering

The agent answers the call and texts the guest a link to your online ordering page. It's simple and works almost anywhere, but the guest still has to finish the order on their phone, and some won't.

2. Orders sent directly into SpotOn

The agent takes the whole order in conversation, including modifiers, the guest's name and a pickup time, and creates the order in SpotOn. It prints or shows on your kitchen display like any other ticket. This is the level that actually turns missed calls into revenue.

3. A menu synced from SpotOn

The agent reads your items, prices and modifiers from SpotOn and picks up changes automatically. Without this, someone has to update the AI's menu by hand every time a price changes or the kitchen runs out of something, and sooner or later a guest hears the wrong answer.

The strongest setups do all three. The simplest test for any vendor: “Can we watch a phone order ring into our SpotOn live during the demo?”

Why SpotOn Restaurants Need Their Own Checklist

  • You probably already have online ordering. Many SpotOn restaurants use SpotOn's own online ordering. A phone agent shouldn't compete with it. It should take the order when the guest wants to talk, and text your ordering link when that's the better fit.
  • Full-service menus are modifier-heavy. Steak temperatures, choice of side, dressing, “light,” “extra” and “on the side” are everyday requests at the kind of restaurants that run SpotOn. The agent has to turn each one into the right modifier, not a note the line might miss.
  • Kitchen names aren't guest names. SpotOn lets you give items a kitchen name and a separate name guests see. A phone agent needs the guest-friendly version, or it will read “CHX SAND SPCY” out loud.
  • The phone isn't just orders. Full-service and bar concepts get a lot of reservation, large-party, private event and “are you open?” calls. The agent should handle those as well as orders, and pass the right ones to a person.
  • Groups mix systems. Your next concept or an acquired location might run Toast or Square. If that's likely, pick a phone agent that works across POS systems so you're not running two phone setups.

Getting Your SpotOn Menu Ready for Phone Orders

A phone agent can only be as clear as the menu it reads. An hour of cleanup in SpotOn before you go live pays off on every call:

  1. Use names guests say out loud. Fill in the guest-facing name for every item: “Spicy Chicken Sandwich,” not “CHX SAND SPCY.”
  2. Clean up modifier groups. Give each group a clear name like “Choose Your Side,” and remove duplicates and old groups no one uses.
  3. Mark required choices as required. If an item can't be made without a temperature or a side, make that group required in SpotOn.
  4. Put prices on paid modifiers. Add avocado, a size upgrade or a premium side should carry its own price, so phone orders total correctly.
  5. List allowed substitutions as options. If fries can become onion rings for $1, that should be an option in the side group, not something only your staff know.
  6. Hide what you don't sell to go. Dine-in-only items, bottle service, staff meals and test items shouldn't be offered on the phone.
  7. Keep 86'd items current. When you run out of something, mark it in SpotOn so a synced agent stops offering it.
  8. Decide pickup times by daypart. Fifteen minutes at 3 PM might be forty at 7 PM on a Saturday. Tell the agent what to promise.
  9. Write down your top ten questions. Hours, parking, happy hour, gluten-free options, large parties, private events. Clear answers here handle most calls that aren't orders.

Features That Matter for SpotOn Restaurants

Every call answered, all at once

Your busiest hour in the dining room is usually your busiest hour on the phone too. Look for an agent that answers unlimited calls at the same time, so nobody hears a busy signal, and that answers 24/7, including after hours and holidays.

Ordering that handles your modifiers

Guests don't order from a script. They say “two burgers, one medium, one well, and no onions on the well one.” The agent should handle combos, substitutions and changes mid-order, then read the order back before sending it.

Upselling that sounds natural

Suggesting an appetizer, a dessert or a bottle of wine is the first thing that gets skipped during a rush. An AI agent can make a relevant suggestion on every order, and you should be able to choose items to promote.

Reservation and large-party calls

For full-service restaurants, a big share of calls are about tables, not takeout. The agent should handle reservation and waitlist questions, and send private event and large-party requests to the right manager with the details already captured.

Smooth handoff to your team

Some calls need a person: a complaint, a large catering request or a regular who wants the manager. Look for a warm handoff, where the agent tells your team member why the guest is calling before connecting them.

Payments without card numbers over the phone

Reading a card number aloud over a loud bar is slow and risky. An agent that texts the guest a secure payment link keeps card numbers off the phone entirely.

Spanish support

In many markets a real share of callers prefer Spanish. An English-only agent quietly loses those orders.

Reporting you'll actually use

You should see how many calls came in, what guests asked about, how many became orders and how upsells performed, by location if you run more than one. Automatic call tagging (complaints, missing items, catering, reservations) shows patterns you'd never spot from behind the host stand.

What to Test in a Demo

Don't settle for a recorded sample call. Ask the vendor to use your menu and try these:

  1. Order your most complicated item, with a temperature, a side swap and an add-on.
  2. Order two of the same item with a change to only one.
  3. Change your mind mid-order: add an item, remove one, change a size.
  4. 86 an item in SpotOn beforehand, then try to order it.
  5. Open the ticket in SpotOn and check the items, modifiers, notes, pickup time and guest name.
  6. Ask about a table for eight on Saturday, and listen to what happens next.
  7. Place an order in Spanish.
  8. Ask for a manager and listen to the handoff.

Pricing Models Compared

Pricing is where AI phone agents differ the most, and where surprises show up later.

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Cheap when it's slow, expensive when it works.
  • Per minute or per call: your bill rises on your busiest days, which can push you to limit how much you use it.
  • Flat monthly fee per location: one price no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget, and every extra order is yours to keep.

Whatever you're comparing, ask for the total cost at your real call volume, including setup fees, call caps and charges for extra locations. Ask whether there's a discount for paying annually, too. Our guide to what AI phone answering costs walks through the math.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Slang AI, ConverseNow, SoundHound, Palona AI or anyone else, these questions separate real SpotOn support from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go directly into SpotOn, or does the guest get a link?
  2. Does the menu sync from SpotOn automatically, including 86'd items, and how fast do changes show up?
  3. How are modifier groups, required choices, combos and substitutions handled?
  4. Can we watch a phone order ring into our SpotOn live in the demo?
  5. How do you handle reservation, large-party and private event calls?
  6. What happens when a guest asks for a person? Does the agent brief my team first?
  7. Is there a limit on calls, or extra charges when we're busy?
  8. Do you take a percentage of my orders?
  9. Does it handle Spanish?
  10. Who does setup, and what do you need from us?
