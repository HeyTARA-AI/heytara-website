Best AI Phone Agent for Olo Restaurants (October 2026)

If your brand runs on Olo, you've already done the hard part of digital ordering. Web, app and delivery orders flow into the POS at every location, menus stay consistent, and corporate can see what's selling. The phone is the exception. At most multi-unit brands it still depends on whoever is free to pick up at each store, and nobody at headquarters knows how many calls went unanswered last Friday.

An AI phone agent fixes that by answering every call and sending phone orders down the same path as your other digital orders. But “works with Olo” means different things to different vendors, and the difference matters more when you have 20, 50 or 200 locations. This guide covers what a real Olo connection looks like, the multi-location details to test, how pricing scales and the questions to ask before you sign.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for Olo Brands?

The best AI phone agent for a restaurant brand on Olo does five things well:

  1. Sends phone orders through your Olo connection to the POS, so they reach the kitchen like any other digital order, with no re-keying.
  2. Syncs the menu for every location automatically, including local prices, modifiers and items a store has run out of.
  3. Handles catering and large orders instead of losing them to a missed call.
  4. Routes calls and reports by location, so corporate and each general manager see what's happening on the phone.
  5. Charges a price that scales sensibly as you add stores, without taking a cut of every order.

Why Olo Brands Have Different Needs

A single-location restaurant needs a phone agent that knows one menu and one set of hours. A brand on Olo usually needs much more:

  • Many locations, one standard. Every store should answer the same way, in the brand's voice, with the same upsells and the same policies.
  • Local differences. Prices, hours, pickup times and availability often vary by store, and the agent has to follow each one.
  • Catering. For many fast casual brands, catering is a large share of revenue, and big orders tend to start with a phone call.
  • Visibility. Operations teams want to know how many calls each store gets, how many turn into orders and what guests are asking for.
  • Sometimes more than one POS. Restaurant groups that have grown through acquisitions or run several brands often have different systems in different places.

A phone tool that was built for a single independent restaurant can struggle with all five. Test for them specifically.

What “Works With Olo” Should Actually Mean

Ask every vendor which of these three levels they actually deliver.

1. Text-to-order links

The agent answers, then texts the guest a link to your Olo-powered online ordering page. It's better than a missed call, but the guest has to finish the order on their phone, and some won't.

2. Orders routed to the POS

The agent takes the whole order in conversation, including modifiers, special instructions, the guest's name and pickup time, and sends it through the Olo connection to the POS at the right location. The kitchen sees it like any other digital order. This is the level that turns calls into revenue.

3. Real-time menu sync for every store

The agent reads the menu for each location and keeps up when it changes. When a store runs out of something or you roll out a new limited-time item, callers hear the change without anyone updating a second system.

The strongest setups do all three: direct ordering, a text link as a fallback for guests who prefer it, and menus that always match what each store is actually selling.

Multi-Location Details Worth Testing in a Demo

This is where weak integrations break. Ask to test with your real menu and at least two of your locations:

  • Location-specific menus and prices: call two stores with different pricing and check that each ticket has the right numbers.
  • Out-of-stock items: mark something unavailable at one store, then try to order it. The agent should offer an alternative, not take the order.
  • Modifiers: order your most customized item, with substitutions and removals, and read the ticket that lands in the POS.
  • Store hours and pickup times: call one location after it closes and confirm the agent doesn't accept an order it can't fill.
  • Transfers by location: ask for a manager and check that the call goes to that store, not a central number nobody answers.
  • Reporting: can you see calls, orders and common questions by location and by brand?
  • New stores: how long does it take to add a location once you open it?

Don't Forget Catering

Catering orders are usually the biggest tickets a store takes, and they're the easiest to lose. They often come in during the lunch rush, need questions answered about headcount, timing and delivery, and depend on someone remembering to call back.

A good AI phone agent should handle catering the way you want: take the order on the call, send the request straight to your catering team by phone or email, or do both. Ask how catering calls are flagged and who gets notified. If catering matters to your brand, make it part of the demo.

Pricing Models Compared

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Across dozens of stores, that adds up fast, and it grows every time the agent does its job.
  • Per minute or per call: your bill climbs on your busiest days and at your busiest stores, which makes budgeting by location harder.
  • Flat monthly fee per location: one predictable price per store, no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget across a portfolio, and every extra order is upside.

Whatever the model, ask for the total at your real call volume across all your locations, including setup fees, call caps and any charges for catering or transfers. Our guide to what AI phone answering costs walks through the math.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Palona AI, SoundHound, Slang AI, ConverseNow or anyone else, these questions separate a real Olo connection from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go to our POS through Olo, or does the guest just get a link?
  2. Does the menu sync automatically for every location, including local prices and out-of-stock items?
  3. How do you handle different hours and pickup times by store?
  4. How do you handle catering and large orders?
  5. When a guest asks for a person, which phone does the call go to?
  6. What reporting do we get by location and by brand?
  7. Is there a cap on calls, or extra charges when we're busy?
  8. Do you take a percentage of our orders?
  9. What if some of our locations or brands use a different POS?
  10. Who handles setup, and how long until our locations are live?
