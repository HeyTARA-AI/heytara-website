Best AI Phone Agent for Square Restaurants (October 2026)

If your restaurant runs on Square, you already have a modern POS, online ordering and a menu that's easy to update. What most Square restaurants don't have is a reliable way to answer the phone during the dinner rush. Calls ring out, guests hang up, and those orders go to whoever picks up next.

An AI phone agent fixes that by answering every call, answering common questions and taking orders that go straight into Square. But not every "Square integration" works the same way, and pricing models vary a lot. This guide walks through what to look for, what to avoid and the questions to ask before you sign up.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for Square?

The best AI phone agent for a Square restaurant does five things well:

  • Sends phone orders directly into Square, not just a text link the guest might ignore.
  • Syncs your Square menu in real time, so 86'd items and price changes never reach a guest.
  • Upsells on every order based on what the guest already picked.
  • Hands off to a person smoothly when a guest asks for one.
  • Charges a predictable price that doesn't grow every time you get busier.

What "Square Integration" Should Actually Mean

Vendors use the word "integration" loosely. Before you compare features, find out which of these three levels a vendor actually supports:

1. Text-to-order links

The agent answers the call, then texts the guest a link to your Square Online ordering page. It's simple and works for almost any restaurant, but the guest still has to finish the order on their phone, and some won't.

2. Direct order entry into Square

The agent takes the full order in conversation, including modifiers, combos and pickup time, and sends it into Square as a ticket. Your kitchen sees it exactly like an in-store or online order. This is the level that actually recovers revenue from missed calls.

3. Real-time menu sync

The agent reads your menu from Square and updates automatically when you change it. Without this, someone has to update the AI's menu by hand every time you change a price or run out of an item, and mistakes reach guests.

The strongest setups combine all three: direct ordering when it's turned on, a text link as a fallback, and a menu that always matches Square.

Features That Matter for Square Restaurants

Answers every call, at the same time

Friday at 6:30 p.m. is when your phone rings most and your staff can answer least. Look for an agent that handles unlimited calls at once, so no guest ever hears a busy signal, and that answers 24/7, including after hours and holidays.

Upselling that sounds natural

Your cashiers know they should suggest a drink or dessert, but during a rush it's the first thing that gets skipped. An AI agent can make a relevant suggestion on every order, like adding fries to a burger order, and you should be able to promote specific items too.

FAQ answers you control

Most calls aren't orders. They're "Are you open?", "Where do I park?" and "Do you have gluten-free options?" The agent should answer these from information you provide and let you edit the answers yourself, without waiting on a support ticket.

Smooth handoff to your team

Some calls need a person: a catering order, a complaint or a regular who wants the manager. Look for routing by intent (manager, catering, a specific store) and a warm handoff, where the agent tells your staff member why the guest is calling before connecting them.

Spanish support

In many markets a real share of callers prefer Spanish. An English-only agent quietly loses those orders.

Reporting you'll actually use

You should be able to see how many calls came in, what guests asked about, how many became orders and how upsells performed. Automatic call tagging (complaints, missing items, catering, hours) shows you patterns you'd never spot from the floor.

Pricing Models Compared

This is where AI phone agents differ the most, and where costs can surprise you later.

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Cheap when you're slow, expensive when it works. The better the agent performs, the more you pay.
  • Per minute or per call: predictable per call, but your bill rises on your busiest days and you may end up limiting how much you use it.
  • Flat monthly fee: one price no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget, and every extra order the agent captures is pure upside for you.

Whatever you choose, ask for the total cost at your real call volume, including any setup fees, call caps or charges for extra locations.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Slang AI, ConverseNow, SoundHound, Palona AI or anyone else, these questions separate real Square support from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go directly into Square, or does the guest get a link?
  2. Does the menu sync from Square automatically, and how fast do changes show up?
  3. How are modifiers, combos and substitutions handled?
  4. What happens when the guest asks for a person? Can the agent brief my staff first?
  5. Is there a limit on calls per month, or extra charges when we're busy?
  6. Do you take a percentage of my orders?
  7. Can I edit FAQ answers myself?
  8. Does it handle Spanish?
  9. What reporting do I get, and can I export it?
  10. How long does setup take, and who does it?
