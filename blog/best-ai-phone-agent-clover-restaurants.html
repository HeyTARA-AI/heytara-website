Best AI Phone Agent for Clover Restaurants (November 2026)

Clover runs the counter at a lot of independent restaurants and growing local groups. It's quick to set up, it handles payments well, and Clover Online Ordering gives guests a way to order without calling. But plenty of guests still pick up the phone, and they call at the worst times: the lunch rush, the Friday dinner push, the ten minutes before close. When nobody answers, that order goes to the restaurant down the street.

An AI phone agent answers those calls, handles the questions and can send the order straight into Clover. This guide covers what a real Clover integration looks like, how to get your Clover menu ready for phone orders, what to test in a demo, how pricing works and the questions to ask any vendor before you sign.

Quick Answer: What Makes the Best AI Phone Agent for Clover?

The best AI phone agent for a Clover restaurant does five things well:

  • Sends phone orders directly into Clover as tickets your kitchen already knows how to read.
  • Stays in sync with your Clover menu, so price changes and sold-out items never reach a guest.
  • Gets your modifiers right: sizes, add-ons, substitutions and “no onions.”
  • Answers every call, including several at once, and hands off to a person smoothly when needed.
  • Charges a flat, predictable price per location, not a cut of every order.

What “Works With Clover” Should Actually Mean

Almost every vendor says they work with Clover. Ask which of these three levels they actually run for Clover restaurants today, not on a roadmap:

1. A link to your online ordering

The agent answers the call and texts the guest a link to your online ordering page, such as Clover Online Ordering. It's simple and works almost anywhere, but the guest still has to finish the order on their phone, and some won't.

2. Orders sent directly into Clover

The agent takes the whole order in conversation, including modifiers and a pickup time, and creates the order in Clover. Your team sees it like any other ticket. This is the level that actually turns missed calls into revenue.

3. A menu synced from Clover

The agent reads your items, prices and modifiers from Clover and picks up changes automatically. Without this, someone has to update the AI's menu by hand every time a price changes, and sooner or later a guest hears the wrong one.

The strongest setups do all three. The simplest test for any vendor: “Can we watch a phone order ring into our Clover live during the demo?”

Why Clover Restaurants Need Their Own Checklist

  • There's often no IT team. Many Clover restaurants are run by owners and managers who already wear every hat. A phone agent needs to be set up for you and then stay out of the way, not become another system to babysit.
  • Your menu lives in modifier groups. Clover menus are built from items, categories and modifier groups. If those are messy, with duplicate groups or vague names like “Add 1,” a phone agent will read them out just as awkwardly as they look on the screen.
  • Admin access matters during setup. Connecting a new app or integration to Clover usually needs the account owner or an admin. Know who that is before onboarding starts, so setup doesn't stall waiting on a login.
  • Growing groups mix systems. Your second concept or newest location might run Toast or Square. If that's likely, pick a phone agent that works across POS systems so you're not running two phone setups.

Getting Your Clover Menu Ready for Phone Orders

A phone agent can only be as clear as the menu it reads. An hour of cleanup in Clover before you go live pays off on every call:

  • Use names guests say out loud. “Chicken Caesar Wrap,” not “CHX CSR WRP.”
  • Clean up modifier groups. Give each group a clear name, mark which choices are required (size, protein) and which are optional, and remove duplicates.
  • Put prices on the modifiers. Extra cheese or a size upgrade should carry its own price, so phone orders total correctly.
  • Hide what you don't sell to go. Dine-in-only items, staff meals, open items and test items shouldn't be offered on the phone.
  • Keep sold-out items current. When you run out of something, mark it in Clover so a synced agent stops offering it.
  • Decide pickup times by daypart. Fifteen minutes at 3 PM might be thirty at 6:30 PM. Tell the agent what to promise.
  • Write down your top ten questions. Hours, parking, gluten-free options, catering, large orders. Clear answers here handle most calls that aren't orders.

Features That Matter for Clover Restaurants

Every call answered, all at once

Your busiest hour for walk-ins is usually your busiest hour on the phone too. Look for an agent that answers unlimited calls at the same time, so nobody hears a busy signal, and that answers 24/7, including after hours and holidays.

Ordering that handles your modifiers

Guests don't order from a script. They say “a large, no tomatoes, and actually make it two.” The agent should handle combos, substitutions and changes mid-order, then read the order back before sending it.

Upselling that sounds natural

Suggesting a drink or a side is the first thing that gets skipped during a rush. An AI agent can make a relevant suggestion on every order, and you should be able to choose items to promote.

FAQ answers you control

Most calls aren't orders. You should be able to edit the agent's answers yourself and see the change right away, without filing a support ticket.

Smooth handoff to your team

Some calls need a person: a complaint, a large catering request or a regular who wants the manager. Look for a warm handoff, where the agent tells your team member why the guest is calling before connecting them.

Payments without card numbers over the phone

Reading a card number aloud at a loud counter is slow and risky. An agent that texts the guest a secure payment link keeps card numbers off the phone entirely.

Spanish support

In many markets a real share of callers prefer Spanish. An English-only agent quietly loses those orders.

Reporting you'll actually use

You should see how many calls came in, what guests asked about, how many became orders and how upsells performed. Automatic call tagging (complaints, missing items, catering, hours) shows patterns you'd never spot from behind the counter.

What to Test in a Demo

Don't settle for a recorded sample call. Ask the vendor to use your menu and try these:

  1. Order your most complicated item, with several modifiers.
  2. Change your mind mid-order: add an item, remove one, change a size.
  3. Change a price or mark an item sold out in Clover beforehand, and see whether the agent knows.
  4. Open the ticket in Clover and check the items, modifiers, notes, pickup time and guest name.
  5. Place an order in Spanish.
  6. Ask for a manager and listen to the handoff.
  7. Ask something that isn't on the menu, like parking or allergens.

Pricing Models Compared

Pricing is where AI phone agents differ the most, and where surprises show up later.

  • Commission per order: you pay a percentage of every order the agent takes. Cheap when it's slow, expensive when it works.
  • Per minute or per call: your bill rises on your busiest days, which can push you to limit how much you use it.
  • Flat monthly fee per location: one price no matter how many calls or orders. Easiest to budget, and every extra order is yours to keep.

Whatever you're comparing, ask for the total cost at your real call volume, including setup fees, call caps and charges for extra locations. Ask whether there's a discount for paying annually, too. Our guide to what AI phone answering costs walks through the math.

Questions to Ask Every Vendor

Whether you're comparing HeyTARA, Loman AI, Slang AI, ConverseNow, SoundHound, Palona AI or anyone else, these questions separate real Clover support from a sales pitch:

  1. Do phone orders go directly into Clover, or does the guest get a link?
  2. Does the menu sync from Clover automatically, and how fast do changes show up?
  3. How are modifier groups, combos and substitutions handled?
  4. Can we watch a phone order ring into our Clover live in the demo?
  5. What happens when a guest asks for a person? Does the agent brief my team first?
  6. Is there a limit on calls, or extra charges when we're busy?
  7. Do you take a percentage of my orders?
  8. Can I edit FAQ answers myself?
  9. Does it handle Spanish?
  10. Who does setup, and what do you need from us?
